### [1] [Exploring LSST's capabilities for early detection of outbursts in low-mass X-ray binaries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26115)
*Susmita Sett,Arash Bahramian,Kristen Dage,David Russell,William I. Clarkson*

Main category: astro-ph.HE

TL;DR: This paper evaluates LSST's ability to detect early optical signals of low-mass X-ray binary outbursts, finding that planned survey parameters pose challenges, though monitoring can still contribute to understanding the broader LMXB population.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To utilize early optical detection of X-ray binary outbursts to better understand accretion disk properties and provide early warnings before X-ray detection.

Method: Assessing the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's LSST capabilities by analyzing survey cadence, filter-specific depth, and observational factors through an extended metric evaluating outburst detectability and recovery fraction.

Result: The currently planned LSST survey strategy makes early detection of X-ray binary outbursts challenging despite the inherent potential for such early optical detection.

Conclusion: Current LSST survey plans limit its effectiveness in early detection of low-mass X-ray binary outbursts, but monitoring through LSST can still inform on the wider population of such binaries in the Galaxy over time.

Abstract: Following long periods of quiescence, low-mass X-ray binaries can exhibit intense X-ray outbursts triggered by instabilities within the accretion disk. These outbursts can sometimes be detected in optical wavelengths before being detected in X-rays, acting as an early onset warning and enabling a deep study of accretion disk properties informed by the lag between optical and X-ray rise. We explore the potential of Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) to detect these outbursts early through optical observations. We evaluate the capabilities of LSST based on currently planned survey cadence, filter-specific depth, and other observational factors that affect early detection. We develop and apply an extended metric to assess outburst detectability and recovery fraction. We find that despite inherent potential for early detection of XRB outbursts, the currently planned survey strategy makes it challenging to detect early onset of XRBs. Lastly, we demonstrate how this estimate can be used to infer the wider LMXB population in the Galaxy as the LSST progresses.

### [2] [Inherent self-consistency of the electron fraction between neutrino-dominated accretion flows and their progenitors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26163)
*Rui-Qi Cui,Tong Liu*

Main category: astro-ph.HE

TL;DR: This study analyzes electron fraction distributions in NDAFs around stellar-mass black holes, showing distinct signatures corresponding to GRB progenitors from collapsars or mergers, and supports NDAFs as GRB engines.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand the electron fraction distribution in neutrino-dominated accretion flows (NDAFs) around stellar-mass black holes as central engines of gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) and how it correlates with GRB progenitors.

Method: Investigated the electron fraction distribution in NDAFs, considering variables including accretion rates, black hole spins, and outflow rates, both with and without disk outflows.

Result: Found that at low accretion rates with massive disks, electron fractions are around 0.5 indicating advection-cooled disk boundaries relevant to collapsar progenitors; at high accretion rates with lower-mass disks, efficient neutrino cooling yields lower electron fractions (~0.38), consistent with compact object merger scenarios; these results hold independent of BH spin.

Conclusion: The electron fraction properties at outer boundaries of NDAFs align with expectations from GRB progenitors, thereby supporting NDAFs as viable central engines of GRBs.

Abstract: Stellar-mass black holes (BHs) surrounded by neutrino-dominated accretion flows (NDAFs) are a leading central engine of gamma-ray bursts (GRBs). In this work, we investigate the electron fraction distribution in NDAFs with or without disk outflows for different accretion rates, BH spins, and outflow rates. As the results, for the cases of the massive disks at relatively low accretion rates, the outer boundary of the disks are predominantly advection-cooled, yielding electron fractions of \(Y_{\rm e} \sim 0.5\), as expected for massive collapsar progenitors. By contrast, in the cases of lower-mass disk at high accretion rates, neutrino cooling becomes highly efficient and mildly electron-degenerate disks emerge, characterized by \(Y_{\rm e} \lesssim 0.38\) at the outer boundary of the disk, even for the strong outflows, which is consistent with materials from compact object merger scenarios. Moreover, we find that these trends remain robust across different BH spins. Consequently, the self-consistent agreement between the electron fraction properties at the outer boundaries of NDAFs and those expected from GRB progenitors provides effectively support for NDAFs serving as the GRB central engines.

### [3] [Single-Pulse Study of the Pseudo-nulling Pulsar PSR J1820-0509 Based on FAST Observations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26171)
*Zefeng Tu,Rushuang Zhao,Hui Liu,Biping Gong,D. Li,P. Wang,Chenchen Miao,Q. J. Zhi,S. J. Dang,S. D. Wang,Q. Zhou,Z. J. Zhang,Xu Zhu,R. W. Tian,H. W. Xu,Yi Zhou,D. Y. Yan*

Main category: astro-ph.HE

TL;DR: This study uses FAST observations to analyze PSR J1820-0509, revealing a very high nulling fraction, multiple emission modes, and weak emission during nulls, suggesting that nulling arises from transitions between different magnetospheric states rather than complete emission shutdown.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To investigate the nulling behavior and emission properties of the high-nulling pulsar PSR J1820-0509 with improved sensitivity and resolution to better understand the nature of pulsar nulling and magnetospheric state changes.

Method: Single-pulse analysis of PSR J1820-0509 was performed using two observations from the FAST telescope, including measurement of nulling fraction, pulse energy distributions, clustering of burst activities, identification of emission modes, and analysis of pulse morphology, polarization, and energy statistics.

Result: An exceptionally high nulling fraction (~81.78%) was measured, showing a bimodal pulse energy distribution with weak residual emission during nulls, identification of three distinct emission modes plus a pseudo-null state with systematic differences, and quasi-periodic clustering of bursts, indicating multiple quasi-stable magnetospheric states governing emission.

Conclusion: The radio emission of PSR J1820-0509 is characterized by multiple quasi-stable magnetospheric states, with nulling representing transitions between different levels of magnetospheric activity rather than complete emission cessation.

Abstract: Using two observations obtained with the Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical radio Telescope (FAST), we present a detailed single-pulse analysis of the high-nulling pulsar PSR J1820-0509. We measure an exceptionally high nulling fraction of approximately 81.78%, significantly exceeding previous estimates from Parkes observations. The single-pulse energy distribution exhibits a clear bimodal structure, consistent with classical nulling behavior. However, stacking the identified null pulses reveals a statistically significant residual profile above the noise level, indicating that the nulls correspond to a very weak emission state rather than a complete cessation of radio emission.
  The pulsar shows clustered burst activities spanning several hundred rotation periods, with prominent quasi-periodicities at 1191 +/- 81 and 590 +/- 15 pulse periods in the two observations. Based on temporal clustering and integrated profile morphology, we identify three distinct emission modes (A, B, and C) and a pseudo-null state (D). These modes exhibit systematic differences in pulse morphology, polarization, and energy statistics. The pulse width-energy relations reveal clear transitions between low- and high-energy regimes. The energy distributions of Modes A and C are well described by lognormal functions, while Mode B follows a composite Gaussian-lognormal distribution.
  These results suggest that the radio emission of PSR J1820-0509 is governed by multiple quasi-stable magnetospheric states. The presence of weak emission during pseudo-nulls, together with systematic mode-dependent variations, supports the interpretation that pulsar nulling reflects transitions between different magnetospheric activity levels rather than a complete shutdown of emission.

### [4] [Challenges in Binary Pulsar Timing Detection of Dark Matter Subhalos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26196)
*Zheng-Long Wang,Zi-Qing Xia,Yue-Lin Sming Tsai,Yi-Zhong Fan*

Main category: astro-ph.HE

TL;DR: A detailed analytical study shows that detecting dark matter subhalos near the solar system using binary pulsar timing is extremely unlikely with current and near-future data, due to low subhalo survival and minimal observable effects.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To evaluate the feasibility of using binary pulsar timing as a probe to detect dark matter subhalos in the solar neighborhood, particularly those with masses around 10^7 solar masses.

Method: Developing a comprehensive analytical framework incorporating the subhalo mass function, projection effects of line-of-sight acceleration, and geometric requirements for joint detection by binary pulsar systems, and applying this framework to current binary pulsar timing data and residual analysis.

Result: The probability of detecting at least one dark matter subhalo within the effective volume using existing binary pulsar data is very low (≤1.6 × 10⁻⁴), with no statistically significant excess accelerations observed. This sets stringent constraints on the detectability of subhalos below 10⁸ solar masses and suggests low survival probabilities for such subhalos near the solar neighborhood. Future observations with the Square Kilometre Array also show a low detection prospect.

Conclusion: Current binary pulsar timing data and analytical methods indicate that detecting dark matter subhalos with masses below 10⁸ solar masses in the solar neighborhood is highly unlikely, consistent with theoretical models of low subhalo survival. Even advanced future measurements are unlikely to significantly improve detection prospects.

Abstract: Recently, binary pulsar timing has been proposed as a viable probe of dark matter subhalos with masses of $\sim 10^7\,M_{\odot}$ in the solar neighborhood. We present a comprehensive analytical framework that incorporates the subhalo mass function, projection effects of line-of-sight acceleration, and the spatiotemporal geometric requirements for joint detection by binary systems, enabling a quantitative evaluation of the detectability of nearby subhalos. Applying this framework to the current binary pulsar sample, we find a probability $\leq 1.6 \times 10^{-4}$ of detecting at least one subhalo within the effective volume. An independent timing residual analysis shows no statistically significant excess in line-of-sight accelerations beyond predictions from data-driven Galactic gravitational potential models. These results place stringent constraints on detecting $<10^8~M_{\odot}$ dark matter subhalos with existing pulsar timing data, aligning with the theoretical expectation that such subhalos have a low survival probability in the solar neighborhood. A low detection prospect still holds even for future Square Kilometre Array observations.

Main category: astro-ph.HE

TL;DR: This paper analyzes gravitational-wave signals from supernova simulations, revealing that a characteristic 'power gap' frequency correlates with neutron star core properties and can be explained by interference between oscillation modes, informing on matter at extreme densities.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand the origin and physical implications of the 'power gap' in the gravitational-wave spectrum emitted by core-collapse supernovae.

Method: Conducted 60 two-dimensional core-collapse supernova simulations spanning various progenitors and equations of state, analyzed the frequency characteristics of gravitational-wave emission focusing on the power gap, compared simulation outcomes to proposed theoretical mechanisms, and tested a scenario of destructive interference to explain the gap.

Result: Identified strong correlations between the power-gap frequency and inner core properties such as sound speed, supporting the idea that the gap encodes matter behavior at extreme densities; demonstrated that destructive interference between oscillation modes can reproduce the observed power gap.

Conclusion: The gravitational-wave power gap in core-collapse supernovae encodes valuable information about the inner neutron star core and is likely caused by interference effects between oscillation modes and background signals, offering a probe into matter under extreme conditions.

Abstract: We analyse the gravitational-wave emission from 60 two-dimensional core-collapse supernova simulations. The models cover a range of progenitors and equations of state. We focus on the narrow frequency interval in the gravitational-wave spectrum where the emitted power is strongly suppressed (the power gap) and how its central frequency relates to the physical properties of the simulations. We find that the power-gap frequency exhibits strong and systematic correlations with the properties of the inner core of the forming neutron star, for example the sound speed, suggesting that the gap encodes information about the behaviour of matter at extreme densities. We further examine how well several mechanisms proposed in the literature account for the presence and evolution of the gap in our simulations. Finally, we explore a scenario in which the gap arises from destructive interference between a narrow oscillation mode and a broadband background signal, demonstrating that such an interaction can produce a sharp minimum in the emitted gravitational-wave power.

### [6] [Relativistic Tidal Disruption in Black Hole and Wormhole Backgrounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26442)
*Pritam Banerjee,Kowsona Chakraborty,Niles Mondal,Tapobrata Sarkar*

Main category: astro-ph.HE

TL;DR: This study uses relativistic hydrodynamic simulations of stellar disruptions to show that black holes and wormholes produce distinct tidal disruption outcomes and observable signatures, enabling their differentiation in strong gravity environments.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To distinguish between black holes and wormholes by studying their distinct spacetime geometries through stellar tidal disruption events under strong gravitational fields.

Method: Numerical simulations using a general relativistic smoothed particle hydrodynamics code inspired by the Liptai and Price algorithm, simulating polytropic stars interacting with supermassive Schwarzschild black holes and exponential wormholes.

Result: Black holes cause greater tidal stripping for a given impact parameter and a lower critical impact parameter for full tidal disruption compared to wormholes. Stars in partial disruptions retain larger cores around more massive centers, explained via tidal stresses computed in a Fermi normal coordinate system and stellar compactness measures. Differences in peak fallback rates and other observables suggest ways to distinguish black holes from wormholes.

Conclusion: The distinct tidal disruption signatures and fallback rates of stars near black holes and wormholes provide a viable observational method to differentiate between these compact objects.

Abstract: Black holes (BHs) and wormholes (WHs) are characterized by distinct spacetime geometries, whose differences become pronounced close to the central objects. A useful way to probe such differences is via the dynamics of stellar tidal disruption events in the regime of strong gravity. Here, using a general relativistic smoothed particle hydrodynamics code inspired from an algorithm developed by Liptai and Price, we perform a suite of numerical simulations of solar mass polytropic stars in the background of supermassive Schwarzschild BHs and similar mass exponential WHs. Important differences between the two geometries near the BH event horizon or the WH throat is provided by the distinct outcomes of such events. For a given impact parameter, BH backgrounds lead to greater tidal stripping compared to WHs ones and further, the critical impact parameter, beyond which the star undergoes full tidal disruption is higher for WH backgrounds compared to BHs. We further study the differences in observable peak fallback rates in the two backgrounds. We also provide a quantitative explanation for the tendency of stars in partial tidal disruptions to retain larger cores around more massive centers, by computing tidal stresses in a Fermi normal coordinate system and introducing an appropriate measure of stellar compactness. Finally, we suggest a way to observationally distinguish BH and WH backgrounds, based on the properties of different observables.

### [7] [Primordial Black Hole interpretation of the sub-solar merger event S251112cm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25795)
*Md Riajul Haque,Fabio Iocco,Luca Visinelli*

Main category: astro-ph.CO

TL;DR: This study evaluates the gravitational wave event S251112cm as a merger of primordial black holes with sub-solar masses, concluding that the PBH scenario is viable within current constraints and emphasizing sub-solar mass GW events as promising probes of PBHs and dark matter.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The observation of gravitational wave event S251112cm suggests the existence of compact objects with sub-solar masses, which cannot be explained by standard stellar evolution, motivating the exploration of primordial black holes (PBHs) as an alternative formation channel.

Method: The authors combine analytic estimates of PBH merger rates with current observational constraints on PBH abundance and the sensitivity of the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA (LVK) detectors to compute the probability of observing such an event as a merger of two PBHs with masses 0.1-1 solar masses.

Result: They find that under relaxed constraints, the probability of observing such a PBH merger event is close to unity for PBHs with masses between 0.5 and 1 solar mass, and remains significant (~0.5) under more conservative constraints and for lower masses. This supports the viability of a PBH interpretation for the event within current bounds.

Conclusion: The PBH interpretation of the sub-solar mass gravitational wave event S251112cm is consistent with current observational constraints but not definitive due to astrophysical uncertainties; nonetheless, sub-solar GW events have strong potential as probes for PBHs and their role in dark matter.

Abstract: The LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA (LVK) candidate event S251112cm suggests the presence of at least one compact object with sub-solar masses. Since such objects cannot be produced through standard stellar evolution, this observation provides a potential indication of non-standard formation channels. Primordial black holes (PBHs), formed from the collapse of primordial density fluctuations in the early Universe, are a well-motivated candidate. We investigate the interpretation of S251112cm as the merger of two PBHs with masses in the range 0.1-1$\,M_\odot$. Combining analytic estimates of the PBH merger rate with current observational constraints on their abundance and the sensitivity of LVK searches, we compute the probability of observing such an event. Within a relaxed constraint scenario, the probability reaches unity in the range $M_{\rm PBH} \sim 0.5$-$1\,M_\odot$, while it remains sizable, $\sim \mathcal{O}(0.5)$, in more conservative scenarios and at lower masses. Our results show that a PBH interpretation of S251112cm is viable within current bounds. Owing to the dependence of our results on astrophysical uncertainties, such as those affecting the constraints on the abundance of PBHs, they cannot be regarded as conclusive with respect to the nature of the detected event. At the same time, our analysis highlights the potential of sub-solar gravitational wave events as a probe of PBHs and their contribution to dark matter.

</details>
